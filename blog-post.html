How to build a wedding rental list that actually covers everything

CECaesar Events Team · October 2, 2026 · 8 min read

Most couples start their rental list with chairs and tables, then discover three weeks out that nobody accounted for cocktail-hour seating, a gift table, or shade for the ceremony. Here's the order we work in — and the items people forget.

Start with the guest count, not the furniture

Every number on your rental order traces back to one figure: confirmed guests. Before you price a single item, lock a realistic count and add a 5% buffer for late RSVPs. From there the math is straightforward.

  • Ceremony seating: one chair per guest, plus 4–6 spares at the back.
  • Dining seating: one chair per guest — never reuse ceremony chairs unless you've budgeted a flip crew.
  • Cocktail hour: seating for roughly 30% of guests. People stand and mingle, but they need somewhere to land.
  • Tables: a 60" round comfortably seats 8; a 72" round seats 10 if you're not crowding place settings.

Map the event in zones

Walk the venue and name each area out loud: arrival, ceremony, cocktail, dinner, dancing, back of house. Every zone needs its own line on the rental list, because each one has furniture that exists nowhere else in the event.

The zones people skip

  • Arrival: welcome table, seating chart easel, a place for coats and bags.
  • Gifts and cards: a dedicated table with linen — not a corner of the bar.
  • Back of house: catering prep tables, trash corrals, a staff break spot out of sight.
  • Kids and elders: a shaded lounge pocket makes a longer reception far more comfortable.

Linens, place settings and the details

Linens are where a room goes from "rented" to "designed." Measure the drop: a 120" round cloth on a 60" table gives a floor-length finish; a 132" does the same on a 72". Browse the full range on our linens page and order swatches early — screen colour is never the real colour.

For place settings, count per guest and then add 10%. Breakage happens, and a replacement delivery on the day of is the most expensive delivery you'll ever book.

Build the weather plan before you need it

If any part of the event is outdoors, you need a documented rain call: who decides, by when, and what changes. Tents book out fastest in peak season, so reserve the structure even if you expect perfect weather — the hold costs far less than a last-minute scramble.

  • Set the rain-call deadline at 24–48 hours before the event.
  • Confirm whether sidewalls and heating are included or add-ons.
  • Check that your flooring plan works on wet ground.

Lock the logistics timeline

Delivery windows, setup access, and breakdown timing cause more day-of stress than any design decision. Confirm venue load-in hours, elevator or gate access, and exactly when everything must be out. Our crews handle delivery, placement and teardown — but the venue's rules set the clock.

The short version

If you only keep one thing from this article, keep this sequence: confirm the count, map the zones, order furniture per zone, layer in linens and settings, reserve the weather plan, then lock the timeline. Work in that order and nothing important falls through.
